🎮Getting started

Pokémon Idle is an idle/incremental game: battles run automatically and your team keeps fighting while you watch. Pick a region and one of its three starters; every region has its own level 5–75 curve, so a fresh region is a fresh climb.

Your progress saves automatically to this browser — there's nothing to install and no account to make.

🔁The core loop

  • Battle the wild Pokémon on your current route; every win earns EXP and money.
  • Wins on a route unlock the next one — some stairs also want badges, a big enough Pokédex, a team level, or a specific catch.
  • Catch Pokémon to grow your Pokédex (which powers the Shiny Charm).
  • Level your team, beat the region's gyms and league, then open another region.

🗺️Regions & Unlock Tokens

There are nine base regions, each reproducing its real map of towns and routes. You start in the region you picked; the rest are opened with Region Unlock Tokens.

Five post-game expansions sit on top of them — Kanto's Sevii Islands, Galar's Isle of Armor and Crown Tundra, and Paldea's Kitakami and Terarium. They share their parent region's generation and appear as subregions on its Town Map, opening once that region's Champion is beaten — no token needed. Alongside them, around a hundred landmark areas (caves, forests, ruins, towers, lakes, Safari Zones and the Black 2 / White 2 Unova areas) are real wild routes placed between the towns and routes of the base regions.

  • Beating any region's Champion earns a token.
  • Open the Town Map and click a locked region tab (it glows while you hold a token) to unlock it.
  • When a region has nothing left to unlock, the location panel's Next Goal points you at its Champion (to earn the next token) or reminds you to spend a token you already hold.

🔄Prestige

Once you've finished a region (Champion beaten), the Gyms tab offers Prestige: replay the region from the start — routes and badges reset — without losing your Pokémon, items, money or Pokédex. Each prestige raises the region's level and awards Victory Tokens. Its Elite Four reset too, so you must beat them again before the next prestige.

🌿Wild encounters & catching

  • Every route uses its real wild table; the Wild Pokémon grid shows each species, its type, level range and rarity.
  • The world runs on a fixed day/night cycle (one full day every 60 real minutes, shown on the Town Map). Some species only appear by day (Pidgey, Pikachu) or only at night (Hoothoot, Murkrow, Duskull), so a route's finds change with the clock — the encounter index tags them.
  • The ◆ badge marks species that spawn only on that route.
  • Catch settings decide what gets auto-caught: always, only shinies, above a level, or only species new to the Pokédex — set globally or per route.
  • Balls: Poké Ball, Great Ball, Ultra Ball, Premier Ball, the situational Quick / Timer / Repeat / Net / Dive / Dusk / Nest Balls, and the never-failing Master Ball. Each situational ball only gets its catch bonus in the right situation — see What works & what's WIP. Poke, Great and Ultra Balls are the only balls sold in marts; the situational balls are made at the Poke Ball Factory (Laverre City), and Premier Balls come free with every ten balls you buy.

⚔️Battles

  • The familiar type chart, STAB, critical hits, damage variance and stat stages all apply — plus accuracy and evasion stages.
  • Status conditions: poison and toxic deal damage each turn, burn also halves physical attack, freeze and sleep stop a Pokémon acting, paralysis halves Speed and can skip a turn, confusion can make a Pokémon hurt itself, and flinching skips a turn. Leech Seed drains into the planter.
  • Weather (rain, sun, sandstorm, snow, hail) and terrain (electric, grassy, misty, psychic) scale damage, power abilities and add healing, chip damage or status/priority protection each turn. A few routes carry a permanent field weather (Hoenn Route 119 rains, the deserts sandstorm, the snow routes snow), and the Terrain Extender held item stretches a terrain from 5 turns to 8.
  • Abilities and held items are live, but only some are implemented — see What works & what's WIP.
  • Moves are learned by levelling; open a Pokémon's stats and use the Manage Moves panel to re-learn its family's moves for free, or slot in one of its TMs once you have found that TM. If a level-up move arrives while the four slots are full, the oldest level-up move makes room — a TM you taught is never the one forgotten.
  • Use the speed controls to fast-forward, and the catch selector to batch-apply Repel/Honey.
  • Mega Evolution: a Mega-capable Pokémon holding the Mega Stone transforms for the rest of the battle (attackers take the X forme, others the Y forme), then reverts afterwards.
  • Auto-battle smarts: your team always takes its best action and may switch to a teammate when the matchup turns bad. Foes scale with the fight — wild Pokémon and route trainers attack at random, legendary wilds and gym leaders play the full move-picking game, and the Elite Four and Champion play at their best (and the Champion switches too).
  • The Battle Reference lists every move and ability and what the engine does with it.

🏅Gyms, Elite Four & Champion

Each region has eight gyms, an Elite Four and a Champion. Badges gate the roads onward, and the league asks for all eight before it will let you challenge it. Beating a Champion is what opens the next region.

You challenge each of them where they stand: walk into a gym's town and the location panel shows the leader with a FIGHT button, and the Pokémon League itself shows the Elite Four and Champion with BEGIN CHALLENGE. The League tab is the overview — badges, team levels and a GO TO shortcut to travel to a gym or the league you haven't reached yet.

Leaders field much stronger rematch teams once you have every badge in their region, and the league does the same once the Champion is beaten. The Elite Four and Champion are fought as one run, with your team healed before it starts.

🛒Items & shops

  • Poké Marts (in towns) sell balls, potions and repels; their stock opens up as you progress, so Ultra Balls aren't available in the first town.
  • Cafés in the larger cities (Celadon, Goldenrod, Slateport, Jubilife, Castelia, Lumiose, Hau'oli, Motostoke, Mesagoza) sell a Poffin (+30 friendship — handy for friendship evolutions) and a Poké Bean (a small heal).
  • Victory Tokens — earned from gyms, Champions, Prestige and the Battle Tower — are spent in the Kecleon Shop, found in the Backpack's Kecleon Wares tab.
  • The Kecleon Shop stocks evolution items: elemental stones, King's Rock, Metal Coat, Protector, Upgrade, the Link Cable (trade evolutions) and more.
  • Held items (Leftovers, Choice items, Berries, type boosters…) come from Kalos's Grand Auction House and are equipped from the Bag's Held Items tab. Their effects are listed in What works & what's WIP.
  • Marts buy back only Valuables — Nuggets, Pearls, Stardust, Star Pieces, Big Nuggets and Comet Shards. Potions, balls, held items and other gear can't be sold.
  • Hover a move, ability or held item wherever it appears (battle bar, Move Manager, a Pokémon's summary, the party or the Box) for a plain-English explanation of what it does.
  • Repel halves a chosen species' encounter rate; Honey doubles it. Exp. Share levels the whole party.

✨Evolution

Most Pokémon evolve by level, but many need an item: stone evolutions (Pikachu, Eevee, Vulpix, Growlithe, Gloom…), and Pokémon that normally evolve by trading (Kadabra, Machoke, Graveler, Haunter and friends) use a Link Cable.

Open a Pokémon's stats panel to see every evolution it can make and what it needs. Some evolutions need high friendship instead of a level (Golbat → Crobat, Pichu → Pikachu, Eevee's Espeon / Umbreon / Sylveon, and several others) — friendship rises as the Pokémon levels up and wins battles.

🌊Raid Islands

Every region has a floating Raid Island holding the Pokémon you can't find on any of its routes — including its legendaries. One raid at a time, with a short cooldown between attempts.

🥚Daycare & breeding

  • Deposit two Pokémon from their stats panel into the Daycare; they produce an Egg after 50 battles.
  • Breeding respects gender and egg groups: the parents must be opposite genders and share an egg group. Ditto can breed with almost anything, genderless Pokémon need a Ditto, and Undiscovered species (legendaries, babies) won't breed at all.
  • Eggs hatch into the mother's base form at level 1, with 1-in-4,096 shiny odds — improved to 1-in-512 if a parent is shiny. The Egg goes to your party (or box when full) and hatches after 30–120 wild wins depending on the species.
  • Eggs inherit from their parents: 3 of the parents' best IVs, a 50% chance of a parent's nature, and the parents' moves that the hatchling can learn. A parent that knows one of the species' egg moves passes it down too — those are breed-only moves that no level-up or TM can teach, and the hatchling keeps them to re-learn later.

🔗Wonder Trade

Spend a Link Cable on any party or box Pokémon to trade it for a random Pokémon at the same level — with a boosted chance of a shiny. The swap plays out as a short animation before the new Pokémon lands in your box. Avoid it for your last party member (the trade is refused).

Rumour has it the trade gives back something special if you trade away a shiny.

🏰Battle Tower

The Battle Tower lives in Hoenn. Reach Route 111 and its Gyms tab opens the tower: a ladder of increasingly strong trainers for Victory Tokens, with your team healed before each floor. Stats start scaling at floor 15 and the pay is one token per ten floors. Each floor is a self-contained puzzle — clearing it returns you to the panel so you can prepare for the next, and a loss just means retrying that floor, never replaying the climb. Your best floor is recorded.

Tower battles are a deterministic puzzle: each floor's six opponents are fixed and there are no critical hits, misses or damage variance, so the result comes down to your team's stats, types, moves, abilities and held items. The Gyms tab previews the next floor so you can build a counter before committing. Other regions use their own minigames (see below).

🎲Minigames & activities

Reaching the right place opens that area's minigame in its location panel. Each one takes over the main screen and pauses the idle loop while it runs.

Minigames cheer a good result and buzz a miss (with the Sound Effects setting on). Recent polish: Puzzle League tiles are Pokémon faces that slide, pop and drop as you play; Poké Radar's grass patches show the route's Pokémon; the Bug Catching Contest only judges your best catch for the podium (you keep everything you catch); and the Pokéathlon opens with a countdown and instructions before the first event.

Route activities

  • Fishing on water routes — time the cast bar to hook a Water Pokémon; a perfect cast boosts the shiny odds and level of what you hook. Better rods unlock as you open regions: Old → Good → Super.
  • Foraging in forests and gardens — time the ring for berries and supplies, and a good search can flush out a Grass Pokémon.
  • Rock Smash on cave routes, Headbutt on grassy routes and Diving on water routes find Rock/Ground, Normal/Flying and deep Water Pokémon.
  • Activity finds spawn a few levels above the route, have boosted shiny odds and can hold an item. You see the find before the battle with a BATTLE / LET IT GO choice, and can ATTEMPT CATCH it directly.

Regional minigames

  • Kanto: Celadon City's Game Corner slots, Vermilion City's Whack-a-Diglett and the Safari Zone (four areas — Center, East, North and West — each carrying its own rare Pokémon, and Safari finds roll shiny at twice the usual odds).
  • Johto: Route 35's Bug Catching Contest, Ecruteak City's Pokéathlon, Goldenrod City's Puzzle League and Violet City's Ruins of Alph (slide the stone tiles back into order).
  • Hoenn: Route 111's Battle Tower, Lilycove City's Pokémon Contest, Slateport City's Pokémon Memory and Mauville City's Lights Out.
  • Sinnoh: Eterna City's Underground, Sandgem Town's Poké Radar and Snowpoint City's Snowpoint Temple (an ice-slide maze — slide until a wall stops you; the board sits beside the D-pad).
  • Unova: Nimbasa City's Voltorb Flip, Virbank City's Pokéstar Studios and Castelia City's Pokémon Musical.
  • Kalos: Lumiose City's Auction House, Laverre City's Poké Ball Factory and Kiloude City's Battle Maison (2v2 double battles).
  • Alola: Heahea City's Battle Royal, Hauoli City's Mantine Surf (jump the swell as the rider sweeps across the wave face) and the Poké Pelago hub.
  • Galar: Motostoke's Curry cooking, Hammerlocke's Dynamax Adventures and the Wild Area's Rotom Rally (hold the throttle but keep the motor off the redline).
  • Paldea: Mesagoza's Academy Quiz, Cortondo's Picnic (pick three fillings to lure a wild Pokemon of one of their types with tripled shiny odds) and Kitakami's Ogre Oustin' (move the basket under the falling berries, and mind the rocks).

Double battles (Battle Maison, Kiloude City): two of your Pokémon face two foes at once. Every battler picks its own target, spread moves like Earthquake and Surf hit both foes, and a fainted Pokémon is swapped for the next one on the bench. Ally abilities finally matter here — Battery and Power Spot power up a partner, Friend Guard softens hits on it, and Healer can cure it.

🐾Companion & Poké Pelago

Pin one party Pokémon as your companion (the paw button in the top bar) and keep it fed, happy and rested. Its care fades as you win battles and one care action is allowed per win; a companion with all four care stats at 50+ grants +15% EXP.

In Alola, the Poké Pelago hub (from Hau'oli City) opens as a full screen with Companion, Berry Fields and Islands tabs. Plant Oran, Sitrus or Lum Berries in the garden's four plots and harvest them as they ripen, then blend three Berries into a Poffin (+30 friendship).

  • The Bean Farm, Training Isle and Isle Caves can each be staffed with boxed Pokémon. They gather Poké Beans, EXP and items/treasures while you battle, banking rewards to collect when you return. Collecting the Bean Farm or Isle Caves itemises exactly what you got, and Training Isle workers show an EXP bar that fills when you collect.

🎁Gifts & static encounters

Towns and routes offer one-off NPC gifts (item packs, Eevee, a regional starter, a Riolu…) and fixed static wild battles (a Snorlax blocking the road, a wiggling Sudowoodo, a honey-tree Munchlax…). Each is claimable once per save and is listed in the location panel, with any badge or Pokédex gate spelled out. A static only counts as claimed once you actually catch it.

🌟Shiny hunting

  • Base shiny odds are 1 in 4,096; the Shiny Charm is per generation — completing a generation's national dex shortens its species' odds from 1 in 2,048 for the first completed generation down to 1 in 256 once every generation is done.
  • The shiny chain improves the odds of a species you encounter repeatedly (up to 4× at 120 in a row), and resets when a different species appears; the chained species shows a ✦ badge. The Poké Radar banks chain levels toward a guaranteed shiny instead — each banked level counts toward a target of 5, then 10, 15, …
  • Honey doubles a species' encounter rate and Repel halves one. Once the chain reaches 4 they lock the hunt in: Honey heavily favours the hunted species and Repel pushes every other species away, so a chain can't be broken by a random encounter.

🐝Swarms & roamers

  • Swarms: occasionally one of a route's rarer species becomes very common there for 256 battles. The Wild Pokémon header shows an active swarm.
  • Roaming legendaries: a region's uncaught legends can appear on any of its routes and may flee mid-battle — catch them fast, and once caught they leave the roaming pool.

📊Records & achievements

The Records panel (in the Box) summarises your journey: battles won, Pokédex and shiny progress, badges, Victory Tokens, per-region completion and prestige, and your best Tower floor. It also tracks 42 achievements.

📖Pokédex & Shiny Charm

The Pokédex has tabs per generation. Completing a generation's national dex unlocks the Shiny Charm for that generation's species: shiny odds improve from 1 in 4,096 to 1 in 2,048 for the first completed generation, then tighten toward 1 in 256 once every generation is complete.

🔎Encounter index

Looking for a specific Pokémon? The Wild Encounter Index is a searchable, offline page listing every species, level range and rate for every location across every region. Looking for what a move or ability actually does? The Battle Reference breaks down every one.

📋Battle reference

The Battle Reference is the companion to this guide: a searchable, offline breakdown of every move (with its type, power, accuracy and effect), every ability (with what it does, grouped by effect, and which are still inert) and every held item. It is generated straight from the game data, so it always matches what the engine actually runs.

💾Saves

Your game saves automatically to this browser. Settings (the gear / ? menu) can export your save to a file and import one back, or reset to start over. Cloud sync is planned.

🧪What works & what's WIP

The engine is deep but not complete. The full, auto-generated Battle Reference lists every move, ability and held item and what the engine does with it; this section is the short version.

Moves — what actually does something

A move only works if its effect is one the engine understands. Any move whose effect falls outside the list below behaves like a plain attack (if it has power) or does nothing at all (if it is a status move). Of the 934 moves in the data, about 484 have a recognised effect, 301 more are plain damaging attacks, and the remaining 149 status moves are still cosmetic — see the move list.

  • Damage moves work, including STAB, the type chart, critical hits and the 85–100% damage roll.
  • Stat changes (Swords Dance, Growl, Calm Mind, Agility…), including the accuracy and evasion stages used by Sand Attack / Double Team.
  • Status: poison, badly poisoned (toxic), burn, freeze, sleep, paralysis, confusion, flinching, infatuation (Attract) and Curse.
  • Healing (Recover, Roost, Synthesis, Rest…) and drain moves (Giga Drain, Leech Life).
  • Multi-hit moves (Bullet Seed, Icicle Spear…) and high critical-hit moves (Slash, Stone Edge…).
  • Protect / Detect (and King's Shield-style blocking), Taunt, Substitute, Leech Seed and the stat reset of Haze / Clear Smog.
  • Screens: Reflect, Light Screen, Aurora Veil.
  • Entry hazards: Stealth Rock, Spikes, Toxic Spikes, Sticky Web — they hit Pokémon as they switch in.
  • Weather: Rain Dance, Sunny Day, Sandstorm, Snowscape and Hail.
  • Terrain: Electric, Grassy, Misty and Psychic Terrain.
  • Utility: Wish, Yawn, Encore, Disable, Perish Song, Tailwind, Trick Room, Focus Energy, Aqua Ring, Ingrain, type changes, ability swaps and item tricks.
  • Recoil, crash-on-miss, recharge (Hyper Beam), self-destruct and multi-turn locks (Outrage-style) are handled.
  • TMs: the Manage Moves panel lists the TMs a Pokemon can learn (its real machine move set). TMs are found, not bought — win a gym badge for a type-appropriate TM, find one on a wild win or at a themed minigame, or claim one from an achievement. Once found a TM is reusable and costs a small fee to teach. When every slot is a TM, a new level-up move waits in the Manage Moves panel instead of replacing one.
Not yet implemented: the large tail of one-off utility moves. The ~160 status moves not listed above do little.

Abilities — the implemented set

Every Pokémon rolls one of its real abilities (there is a small chance of the hidden one) and the stats panel shows it. 256 of the 310 abilities now have an effect — entry and weather/terrain setters, type absorbers, offensive and defensive modifiers, speed abilities, status control, contact and on-hit reactions, on-KO rewards, stat protection, form changes and item/berry abilities. The classes of effect are:

  • Entry & field: Intimidate, Drizzle, Drought, Sand Stream, Snow Warning, the four Surges, Hadron Engine, Orichalcum Pulse, Intrepid Sword, Dauntless Shield, Download, Trace, Imposter, Neutralizing Gas, Air Lock, Mold Breaker and friends.
  • Type immunity / absorb: Levitate, Water Absorb, Dry Skin, Volt Absorb, Lightning Rod, Motor Drive, Flash Fire, Well-Baked Body, Sap Sipper, Storm Drain, Earth Eater, Wonder Guard, Soundproof, Bulletproof.
  • Offence: Huge Power, Adaptability, Technician, Tough Claws, Sheer Force, Tinted Lens, Sniper, the pinch abilities (Overgrow, Blaze, Torrent, Swarm), Iron Fist, Strong Jaw, Sharpness, Mega Launcher, Protean / Libero and the move-type changers (Normalize, Pixilate…).
  • Defence: Thick Fat, Multiscale, Shadow Shield, Filter, Solid Rock, Prism Armor, Fluffy, Ice Scales, Fur Coat, Magic Guard, Sturdy, the Ruin quartet.
  • Speed: Speed Boost, Quick Feet, Unburden, and the weather runners (Swift Swim, Chlorophyll, Sand Rush, Slush Rush).
  • Status & weather: Immunity, Limber, Water Veil, Insomnia, Vital Spirit, Sweet Veil, Magma Armor, Comatose, Shed Skin, Hydration, Poison Heal, Rain Dish, Ice Body, Solar Power.
  • Contact & on-hit: Static, Flame Body, Poison Point, Effect Spore, Cute Charm, Rough Skin, Iron Barbs, Gooey, Mummy, Stamina, Justified, Weak Armor, Anger Shell and more.
  • On-KO & crits: Moxie, Chilling / Grim Neigh, Beast Boost, Soul-Heart, Aftermath, Innards Out, Super Luck, Sniper, Battle Armor, Shell Armor, Merciless.
About 54 abilities are still inert — mostly ally abilities beyond the doubles set (Telepathy, Aroma/Flower Veil, Costar, Commander, Hospitality, Symbiosis, Gulp Missile), trapping (Arena Trap, Shadow Tag) and information abilities (Frisk, Forewarn). A few working ones are simplified: type absorbers heal 1/4 max HP instead of raising a stat, and Hustle skips its accuracy penalty. See the full ability list.

Held items

Held items come from Kalos's Grand Auction House (and are held by wild and trainer Pokémon too) and are equipped from the Bag's Held Items tab. These work:

  • Leftovers (heal 1/16 a turn) and Life Orb (+30% damage, 10% recoil).
  • Choice Band / Specs / Scarf (+50% stat, and they now lock the holder into the first move it uses) and Assault Vest (+50% Sp. Def).
  • Mega Stone enables in-battle Mega Evolution for a Mega-capable holder.
  • Eviolite (+50% Def/Sp. Def unless fully evolved), Focus Sash (survive one KO from full HP).
  • Sitrus Berry and Lum Berry.
  • All 18 type boosters (Charcoal, Mystic Water, Magnet, the plates…), giving +20% to their type.

Other things that trip people up

  • Battle Tower is Hoenn-only and is a deterministic puzzle: each floor's opponent is fixed and there are no crits, misses or damage variance. The Gyms tab previews the next floor so you can build a counter. Other regions use their own minigames (see above).
  • Situational balls only get their bonus in the right situation — Quick (one-turn battle), Timer (long battle), Repeat (already in the Pokédex), Net (Water/Bug), Dive (water routes), Dusk (caves/night) and Nest (low level). Auto-catch picks the best one.
  • Honey + Repel become a real hunt once the shiny chain reaches 4: Honey heavily favours the hunted species and Repel pushes every other species away.
  • Friendship (a ❤ badge in the stats panel) rises with levels and wild wins, and gates friendship evolutions such as Golbat → Crobat and Eevee's Espeon / Umbreon / Sylveon.
  • Breeding inherits 3 of the parents' best IVs, a 50% chance of a parent's nature, moves the hatchling can learn, and any egg moves a parent knows.
  • Challenge modes (Settings): Randomizer (random wild species), Level Scaling (wild levels match your active Pokemon), Nuzlocke (a fainted party Pokemon is released for good — your last one is spared) and a Monotype run.
  • Settings → Display switches between animated sprites (the default) and the lighter static pixel art. The Town Map legend names every marker, including the Day-Care, Berry Garden, Battle Tower, Raid Den, Legendary Shrine and Café service markers.
  • Region minigames are covered in full above — each opens from its location's panel and pauses the idle loop while it runs.
